Reorder Nudge Engine · Experiment Readout

Does a reorder nudge actually cause more orders?

A holdout-controlled readout of the rule-based reorder nudge from the PRD, run on synthetic order history calibrated to public food-delivery and recurring-purchase datasets. Every number below comes from the simulated trigger engine's own event log, not from the ground-truth parameters used to generate the data — the same constraint a real analyst would work under.

Headline numbers

Is the effect real?

Incremental orders per user, treatment minus holdout, with a bootstrap 95% confidence interval. A holdout group is what separates “the nudge caused this order” from “this user was ordering anyway.”

The nudge funnel

From a trigger firing to an incremental order, treatment group only.

Which trigger earns its keep

Conversion at each stage of the funnel, by trigger type. Contextual (rainy-day comfort food) nudges convert best per open — day-of-week and cadence nudges reach more people but convert less often per send.

Who benefits, in relative terms

Incremental lift by order-frequency segment, indexed to each segment's own holdout baseline — raw order counts range from under 1/user (dormant) to 30+/user (power), so relative lift is the fair comparison across them.

Where nudges got suppressed before sending

A fired trigger doesn't always become a nudge — these are the guardrails and eligibility checks from the PRD doing their job.
